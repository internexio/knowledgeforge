# Solo-founder confessional posts — keep first-person "I," skip brand "we" in the close

## The rule

In confessional first-person narrative posts from solo founders (LinkedIn personal, personal essays, memoir-style content, "here's what I got wrong" pieces), consistently use first person "I" throughout — from the opener through the close/CTA paragraph. Do NOT pivot to brand "we" for the closing line.

**Rule of thumb: the pronoun of the close should match the pronoun of the opener.** If the opener owns individual authorship, the close should too.

## Why it fails

The narrative arc creates a specific voice. Pivoting to "we" at the close pretends a team the reader doesn't believe exists. Reads as inauthentic voice-drop, breaks the confessional frame the opener established. When the reader has just spent 200 words trusting an "I" narrator's autobiography, a switch to "we're building X" registers as either (a) a corporate ghostwriter surfacing, or (b) the author borrowing team-legitimacy they don't have.

## Grounded example (client-project, 2026-07-06)

Draft opened confessionally:

> "For 15 of my 20 years in B2B marketing, I ran A/B tests the wrong way... I was at Whitepages when it first bugged me... I called it audience fatigue and moved on."

Closing pivoted:

> "That's the whole thing behind what **we're building**. Not a better AI copy tool. A copy tool that reaches the half of your buyers your current copy is missing."

Operator (solo founder) caught immediately: *"I am a solo founder... is we're still ok?"*

Fix:

> "That's the whole thing behind what **I'm building**. Not a better AI copy tool. A copy tool that reaches the half of your buyers your current copy is missing."

## When brand "we" IS appropriate for a solo founder

- Business page posts (brand-authoritative voice, not confessional)
- Company update announcements
- Positioning statements
- Any post where the reader is primed for brand-as-actor from the first sentence

## When brand "we" is INappropriate for a solo founder

- Confessional narratives ("here's what I got wrong")
- Personal essays with autobiographical anchors ("I was at [company] when...")
- First-person present-tense observations ("I've been noticing...")
- Peer-to-peer commiseration posts ("Any founder who's shipped a product knows...")

## Distinction — the inclusive-collective "we"

General industry-scoped "we" ("we [as an industry] were lazy," "we [as marketers] all did this") is fine even in confessional first-person posts. Reads as peer commiseration, not team-implied. It doesn't invoke the writer's company; it invokes the writer's peer group.

The problematic "we" is the **brand/team-implied** "we" ("we're building X"), which suggests a company staffed by more than one person.

## Application in review

On any founder-authored LinkedIn personal or personal essay content, run a **pronoun continuity check**:

1. Read the opener — what pronoun anchors the narrative? (Usually "I" in confessional pieces.)
2. Read the closer — what pronoun carries the CTA or thesis-restate?
3. If they don't match, **the closer usually needs to flip, not the opener.** The opener sets the frame; the close should honor it.

Flag brand-"we" instances in confessional-frame drafts as MEDIUM-to-HIGH severity in review passes. The fix is trivial (single-word swap) but the miss is expensive — it undermines the trust the opener built.

## Cross-cutting

This pattern also applies to:
- Guest posts bylined to a solo founder
- Ghost-written pieces bylined to a solo founder
- Any content where the persona is a single individual by design
